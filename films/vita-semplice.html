Perfect Days

What it's about

The protagonist of Perfect Days lives a humble, repetitive but attentive life. He doesn't want to be seen. He doesn't compete. And he doesn't seem any less alive for it.

First reaction

Better mediocre in other people's eyes, but at peace with myself. Not as resignation, but as a deliberate choice to step off the stage.

What it triggered

Perfection, excellence, grandeur have become default expectations. Contemporary life is performance: a stage where you keep proving your worth, your productivity, your originality. In that system, simplicity looks like failure.

Perfect Days shows an alternative. Not romantic or anti-modern, but concrete: an ordinary life lived with attention. Grandeur isn't the only possible form of a good life. Normality, underrated and often mocked, holds a freedom that the race for "more" can't offer.

Every day, whatever happens, I tell myself: "better mediocre for everyone else, but at peace with me". It isn't always easy to keep in mind, because the pressure to prove yourself is everywhere. But there's a real freedom in not having to be extraordinary. A freedom I found in that normality, so underrated and so feared.
