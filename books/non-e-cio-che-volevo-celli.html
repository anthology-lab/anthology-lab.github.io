Non è ciò che volevo: I principi naturali della crescita personale contro il mito del successo per obiettivi

Central argument

Success by goals, inherited from 1990s coaching (Robbins, Covey, Tracy), measures a life in external results and can give you exactly what you wanted while you lose yourself. Celli proposes a different measure: growth as faithfulness to yourself, that is, how far your actions match who you are. The right question isn't "how do I get what I want?" but "does this choice make me more myself or less?".

The model is nature: growth follows its own rhythms, it isn't a straight climb, it goes down deep like a root. Crises are thresholds, not failures. The book questions three myths of today's self-help: the obsession with results, the forced exit from the comfort zone, and fear treated as an enemy.

Examples / Notable passages

  • The hidden cost to identity: every choice that is right as strategy but false inside builds up a debt paid in "valuta identitaria" ("identity currency"); the goal that becomes your identity is a "gabbia dorata" ("golden cage").

  • Introjected vs integrated motivation (Deci and Ryan): both reach the goals, only the second leaves you whole after reaching them.

  • Performance wears down resilience (Kristin Neff): people who live on results confuse the failed action with a failed self, and the more they win the more fragile they become.

  • Natural principles: known to the spiritual traditions (Taoist wu wei, Marcus Aurelius, Jesus withdrawing from the crowd in Mark, Ignatius of Loyola's discernment) and named today by psychology.

  • Gestures of nature: every chapter ends with small practices to carry the insights from the mind into the body.

  • "Non arrivo dove voglio. Divento chi sono."
    "I don't get where I want. I become who I am."

  • "Il vero successo non si misura da quanto in alto si arriva, ma dalla capacità di non tradirsi mai lungo il tragitto."
    "Real success isn't measured by how high you get, but by being able never to betray yourself along the way."
